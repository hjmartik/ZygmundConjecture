Supporting and additional results

These results complement the three main theorems. Each result links to its independent Lean statement, the definitions it uses and a proof.

We use the setting and notation of the main theorems. The weighted estimates and projection identities use the finite setting defined below.

Square-function estimates

Averages and martingale differences

For a cube \(Q\), let \(\operatorname{ch}(Q)\) be its dyadic children, obtained by halving each side. Define

\[\langle u\rangle_Q=\frac1{|Q|}\int_Q u, \qquad E_Q u=\mathbf1_Q\langle u\rangle_Q, \qquad \Delta_Q u=\sum_{J\in\operatorname{ch}(Q)}E_Ju-E_Qu.\]

On the product space, the operators act in the indicated coordinate. For \(I=I^1\times\cdots\times I^m\), put \(E_I=\prod_iE_{I^i}\) and \(\Delta_I=\prod_i\Delta_{I^i}\), where products of operators mean composition.

Maximal–square-function estimate

Let \(m\geq1\). Define

\[\mathcal M_{\mathcal D}g(x) =\sup_{\substack{I\in\mathcal D\\x\in I}}|\langle g\rangle_I|, \qquad S_{\mathcal D}g(x) =\left(\sum_{I\in\mathcal D}|\Delta_Ig(x)|^2\right)^{1/2}.\]

Here the absolute value in \(\mathcal M_{\mathcal D}\) is taken after the average. Suppose that \(g=\sum_{I\in\mathcal H}\Delta_Ig\) for a finite \(\mathcal H\subset\mathcal D\). Then

\[\|\mathcal M_{\mathcal D}g\|_p \leq 2^{6d+10}\|S_{\mathcal D}g\|_p, \qquad 1\leq p\leq\frac32.\]

Scalar estimate

For \(a\in\mathbb R\) and \(b>0\), set \(\Psi_p(a,b)=a^2/b^{2-p}\). Let \(1<p\leq2\), \(a,h,\eta\in\mathbb R\), and \(b>0\) with \(b+\eta>0\). Then

\[\Psi_p(a+h,b+\eta)-\Psi_p(a,b) -\frac{2ah}{b^{2-p}}+\frac{(2-p)a^2\eta}{b^{3-p}} \geq\frac{p-1}{3-p}\frac{h^2}{\max\{b,b+\eta\}^{2-p}}.\]

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof · Library theorem.

Finite dyadic setting

Let \(m\geq2\). Fix \(I_0\in\mathcal D\) and \(k\in\mathbb Z\) with \(2^{-k}\leq\ell(I_0^i)\) for every \(i\). Set

\[\mathcal D_0^i =\{Q\in\mathcal D^i:Q\subset I_0^i,\ \ell(Q)\geq2^{-k}\}, \qquad (\mathcal D_0^i)^\circ =\{Q\in\mathcal D_0^i:\ell(Q)>2^{-k}\}.\]

The smallest cubes have side length \(2^{-k}\). For a coordinate set \(A\), write \(\mathcal D_0^A=\prod_{i\in A}\mathcal D_0^i\) and \((\mathcal D_0^A)^\circ=\prod_{i\in A}(\mathcal D_0^i)^\circ\), omitting \(A\) when it contains all coordinates. Put \(E_{K^A}=\prod_{i\in A}E_{K^i}\) and \(\Delta_{K^A}=\prod_{i\in A}\Delta_{K^i}\). These operators leave the other coordinates unchanged. For \(A=\varnothing\), both products are the identity operator.

One-coordinate weighted square-function estimate

Fix \(i\); the estimate uses only this coordinate grid. Let \(u,v\) be functions on \(I_0^i\), with \(v>0\), both constant on every \(Q\in\mathcal D_0^i\) with \(\ell(Q)=2^{-k}\). Then

\[\sum_{Q\in(\mathcal D_0^i)^\circ}\int_Q \frac{|\Delta_Q u|^2}{\langle v\rangle_Q^{2-p}} \leq 2^{d_i(2-p)}\frac{3-p}{p-1} \int_{I_0^i}\frac{|u|^2}{v^{2-p}}, \qquad 1<p\leq2.\]

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof · Library theorem.

Product weighted square-function estimate

Let \(f\) be supported on \(I_0\), strictly positive there, and constant on every product of smallest cubes, one of side length \(2^{-k}\) in each coordinate. For every nonempty coordinate set \(A\),

\[\sum_{K^A\in(\mathcal D_0^A)^\circ}\int_{K^A\times I_0^{A^c}} \frac{|\Delta_{K^A}f|^2}{(E_{K^A}f)^{2-p}} \leq 2^{(2-p)\sum_{i\in A}d_i} \left(\frac{3-p}{p-1}\right)^{|A|}\|f\|_p^p, \qquad 1<p\leq2.\]

Here \(A^c\) is the complement of \(A\) among the coordinate factors. The region \(K^A\times I_0^{A^c}\) uses the factor \(K^i\) for \(i\in A\) and \(I_0^i\) otherwise.

Projection identities and estimates

The projection

In the finite dyadic setting, let \(f\) be supported on \(I_0\), strictly positive there, and constant on every product of smallest cubes. Let \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal D_0\) be arbitrary. Define

\[F_{\mathcal G} =f-\sum_{\substack{L\in\mathcal D_0^\circ\\ L\subset I\text{ for some }I\in\mathcal G}}\Delta_Lf.\]

The following projection identities and estimates use this setting.

Preserved averages

If \(\mathcal G\) is weakly incomparable, then

\[E_Rf=E_RF_{\mathcal G},\qquad R\in\mathcal G.\]

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof.

Averages in fewer coordinates

For every \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal D_0\), \(R\in\mathcal G\) and \(x\in R\),

\[E_RF_{\mathcal G}(x) =\sum_{B\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,m\}} (-1)^{m+1+|B|}\left(\prod_{i\in B}E_{R^i}\right)F_{\mathcal G}(x).\]

No incomparability assumption is needed.

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof.

Cross-coordinate averages

For \(j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), put \(A_j=\{1,\ldots,m\}\setminus\{j\}\). For \(K^{A_j}\in\mathcal D_0^{A_j}\), set

\[\mathcal A_{K^{A_j}} =\{I^j:I\in\mathcal G,\ K^{A_j}\subset I^{A_j}\} \cup\{Q^j\in\mathcal D_0^j:\ell(Q^j)=2^{-k}\}.\]

Let \(\mathcal P_{K^{A_j}}\) consist of the maximal cubes of \(\mathcal A_{K^{A_j}}\). These cubes partition \(I_0^j\). Define

\[X_{K^{A_j}}g=\sum_{P^j\in\mathcal P_{K^{A_j}}}E_{P^j}g,\]

acting in coordinate \(j\).

Differences of the projected function

For every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\) and \(K^{A_j}\in\mathcal D_0^{A_j}\),

\[\Delta_{K^{A_j}}F_{\mathcal G} =X_{K^{A_j}}\Delta_{K^{A_j}}f.\]

This is a pointwise identity of functions, with no incomparability assumption.

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof.

Averaging rectangles

In the finite setting, with the cross partitions defined above, put

\[\mathcal B =\bigcup_{j=1}^m \{K^{A_j}\times P^j:K^{A_j}\in\mathcal D_0^{A_j}, P^j\in\mathcal P_{K^{A_j}}\}, \qquad \widehat{\mathcal G}=\mathcal G\cup\mathcal B.\]

The rectangles in \(\mathcal B\) cover \(I_0\).

Unchanged projection and weak incomparability

For every \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal D_0\),

\[F_{\widehat{\mathcal G}}=F_{\mathcal G}.\]

If \(\mathcal G\) is weakly incomparable, then so is \(\widehat{\mathcal G}\).

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof.

Representation of the averages

Suppose \(\mathcal G\) is weakly incomparable. For every \(R\in\widehat{\mathcal G}\) and \(x\in R\),

\[E_Rf(x) =\sum_{B\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,m\}} (-1)^{m+1+|B|}\left(\prod_{i\in B}E_{R^i}\right)F_{\mathcal G}(x).\]

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof.

Square functions of the projection

Write \(F=F_{\mathcal G}\). For \(j=1,\ldots,m\), define

\[S_{A_j}F =\left(\sum_{K^{A_j}\in(\mathcal D_0^{A_j})^\circ} |\Delta_{K^{A_j}}F|^2\right)^{1/2}, \qquad WF=\left(\sum_{j=1}^m(S_{A_j}F)^2\right)^{1/2}.\]

Reduction to square functions

If \(\mathcal G\) is weakly incomparable, then

\[\|M_{\widehat{\mathcal G}}f\|_p \leq C_{m,d}(p')^{m-2}\|f\|_p+C_{m,d}\|WF\|_p, \qquad 1<p\leq\frac32.\]

The constant depends only on \(m\) and \(d\).

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof.

Square-function estimate for the projected function

For every \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal D_0\),

\[\int_{I_0}\frac{(WF)^2}{(M_{\mathcal B}f)^{2-p}} \leq C_{m,d}(p')^{m-1}\|f\|_p^p, \qquad 1<p\leq2.\]

The constant depends only on \(m\) and \(d\). No incomparability assumption is needed.

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof.

Covering, selection and slicing

Prescribed-scale covering

For \(s>0\), set \(\rho(s)=\lceil\log_2s\rceil+2\). In each coordinate factor, use the \(3^{d_i}\) dyadic grids

\[\mathcal D^{i,a} =\{2^k([0,1)^{d_i}+z+(-1)^ka):k\in\mathbb Z,\ z\in\mathbb Z^{d_i}\}, \qquad a\in\{0,1/3,2/3\}^{d_i}.\]

Every closed axis-parallel cube \(Q\subset\mathbb R^{d_i}\) of side length \(s\) is contained in a cube \(P\) from one of these grids with \(\ell(P)=2^{\rho(s)}\).

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof · Library theorem.

Córdoba–Fefferman selection

Let \(\mathcal F\) be a finite family in a product of \(m\geq1\) dyadic cube grids. There is a \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal F\) such that

\[|\mathrm{sh}(\mathcal F)|\leq C_m|\mathrm{sh}(\mathcal G)|, \qquad |I\cap\mathrm{sh}(\mathcal G\setminus\{I\})|\leq\tfrac12|I| \quad(I\in\mathcal G).\]

The constant depends only on the number \(m\) of factors.

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof · Library theorem.

Rounded families and slices

Let \(m\geq3\) and let \(\phi:(0,\infty)^{m-1}\to(0,\infty)\) be coordinatewise nondecreasing. With the rounding rule and shifted grids above, put

\[\Gamma_\phi= \{(\rho(s_1),\ldots,\rho(s_{m-1}),\rho(\phi(s))): s\in(0,\infty)^{m-1}\}.\]

For \(\tau=(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_m)\), with \(\tau_i\in\{0,1/3,2/3\}^{d_i}\), set

\[\mathcal D_\tau=\prod_{i=1}^m\mathcal D^{i,\tau_i}, \qquad \mathcal E_\phi^\tau =\{I\in\mathcal D_\tau: (\log_2\ell(I^1),\ldots,\log_2\ell(I^m))\in\Gamma_\phi\}.\]

For a rectangle family \(\mathcal G\) and \(t\in\mathbb R^{d_m}\), its slice is

\[\mathcal G_t=\{I^1\times\cdots\times I^{m-1}: I\in\mathcal G,\ t\in I^m\}.\]

Weak incomparability of slices

Fix \(\tau\), and let \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal E_\phi^\tau\) be incomparable. For every \(t\in\mathbb R^{d_m}\), the family \(\mathcal G_t\) is weakly incomparable. Each member of \(\mathcal G_t\) is the projection of exactly one \(I\in\mathcal G\) with \(t\in I^m\), and

\[h_{\mathcal G}(x,t)=h_{\mathcal G_t}(x), \qquad x\in\mathbb R^{d_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb R^{d_{m-1}}.\]

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof · Library proofs.

Half-sparsity of slices

Let \(m\geq2\) and let \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal D\) be finite. Suppose that

\[|I\cap\mathrm{sh}(\mathcal G\setminus\{I\})|\leq\tfrac12|I|, \qquad I\in\mathcal G.\]

Then \(\mathcal G_t\) is \(1/2\)-sparse for every \(t\in\mathbb R^{d_m}\).

Endpoint, overlap and sharpness results

These results use the global setting of the main theorems.

Rounded dyadic endpoint estimate

For the rounded family \(\mathcal E_\phi^\tau\), for every \(f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^d)\) and every \(\lambda>0\),

\[|\{x:M_{\mathcal E_\phi^\tau}f(x)>\lambda\}| \leq C_{m,d}\int_{\mathbb R^d}\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda} \left[\log\!\left(e+\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\right]^{m-2}dx.\]

The constant depends only on \(m\) and \(d\), not on \(\phi\) or \(\tau\).

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof · Theorem in the main development.

Dyadic Zygmund conjecture

Let \(m\geq3\) and let \(\Phi:\mathbb Z^{m-1}\to\mathbb Z\) be coordinatewise nondecreasing. Let \(\mathcal Z_\Phi\subset\mathcal D\) be the family of rectangles with side-length tuple

\[\bigl(2^{k_1},\ldots,2^{k_{m-1}}, 2^{\Phi(k_1,\ldots,k_{m-1})}\bigr), \qquad (k_1,\ldots,k_{m-1})\in\mathbb Z^{m-1}.\]

For every \(f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^d)\) and \(\lambda>0\),

\[|\{x:M_{\mathcal Z_\Phi}f(x)>\lambda\}| \leq C_{m,d}\int_{\mathbb R^d}\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda} \left[\log\!\left(e+\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\right]^{m-2}dx.\]

The constant depends only on \(m\) and \(d\), not on \(\Phi\) or the grids.

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof · Library theorem.

Sparse Φ-Zygmund overlap estimate

If \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal Z_\Phi\) is \(\eta\)-sparse with finite shadow, where \(0<\eta\leq1\), then

\[\int_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathcal G)} \exp\!\left(c h_{\mathcal G}^{1/(m-1)}\right)dx \leq C|\mathrm{sh}(\mathcal G)|,\]

where \(c,C>0\) depend only on \(m\), \(d\) and \(\eta\), not on \(\Phi\) or the dyadic grids.

The implication from the Zygmund estimate to this overlap bound is due to Rey.

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof · Theorem in the main development.

Sharpness for the classical dyadic Zygmund family

Let \(m\geq3\), \(0<\eta<1\), and set

\[\Phi(k_1,\ldots,k_{m-1})=k_1+\cdots+k_{m-1}.\]

For every choice of the dyadic grids, there are finite incomparable \(\eta\)-sparse families \(\mathcal G_N\subset\mathcal Z_\Phi\), \(N\geq2\), with \(|\mathrm{sh}(\mathcal G_N)|=1\), such that

\[\lim_{N\to\infty} \int_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathcal G_N)} \exp\!\left(c h_{\mathcal G_N}^{\beta}\right)dx =\infty \quad\text{for every }c>0,\quad\beta>\frac1{m-1}.\]

The same sequence works for every such \(c\) and \(\beta\).

Lean: Independent statement · Definitions · Proof · Theorem in the main development.
