Mathematical objects and their Lean definitions

The specification uses cubes and rectangles, families of rectangles, maximal and overlap functions, and dyadic averaging and difference operators. This guide explains how these objects are represented in Lean and links to their definitions. Where a useful identification requires a proof, that proof is linked alongside the explanation.

The specification guide lists the statements and the definitions needed for each result.

Cubes, rectangles and coordinates

Mathlib's Box records the lower and upper corners of an axis-parallel box. For Q : Box (Fin d), these are the tuples Q.lower and Q.upper, with strictly positive width in each coordinate. The expression (Q : Set (Fin d → ℝ)) denotes the product of the intervals (Q.lower j, Q.upper j].

A Rectangle is a tuple of these boxes, one for each coordinate block of dimension d i. The rectangle families below impose the cube and side-length conditions on its factors. The function rectangle turns that tuple into a subset of \(\mathbb R^d\), where \(d=\sum_i d_i\).

CubeGrid describes a dyadic grid by its cubes at each integer scale: cubes of side \(2^k\) partition the space, and intersecting cubes are nested. gridRectangles consists of all products of cubes from the supplied grids, with the scale chosen independently in each factor.

Point lists all scalar coordinates in one tuple. For operators acting in selected coordinates, ProductPoint groups them into the blocks \(\mathbb R^{d_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb R^{d_m}\); productRectangle describes the same rectangle in these grouped coordinates. The identification is measure preserving, with the corresponding rectangle, integral and norm identities proved in Lean.

appendLastPoint appends the last coordinate block to the first blocks, giving the point \((x,t)\) used in the slicing identities.

Families of rectangles

A family is a set of rectangles, written G : Set (Rectangle d). WeaklyIncomparable means that no two members are strictly nested in every factor. Lean proves the equivalent formulation: whenever one member contains another, at least one factor is unchanged. Incomparable is the stronger condition that no two distinct members contain one another.

Sparse expresses sparseness through pairwise disjoint sets \(E_R\subset R\) with \(|E_R|\geq\eta|R|\). NullMeasurableSet means that these sets are measurable for completed Lebesgue measure.

zygmundRectangles specifies the continuous family: all positions are allowed, the first side lengths are arbitrary positive numbers, and phi gives the last. The code writes n + 1 for the paper's number \(m\) of factors. dyadicZygmundRectangles uses cubes from the given grids, with the last side-length exponent prescribed by Phi.

For the rounded family, rounding is the rule \(\rho(s)=\lceil\log_2 s\rceil+2\). roundedScales retains every tuple obtained by applying this rule to the continuous side lengths. The first rounded scales may occur with more than one last scale. shiftedCubes gives the explicit shifted grids, and roundedRectangles selects their products with these rounded side lengths.

initialProjection retains the first factors of a rectangle. sliceFamily forms \(\mathcal G_t\): it projects the members of \(\mathcal G\) whose last factor contains \(t\), counting each projection once.

Maximal and overlap functions

For a family \(\mathcal G\), shadow is the union of its rectangles and overlap is the sum of their indicators. maximal is the supremum, over rectangles containing the point, of the averages of \(|f|\). Its averages use the nonnegative Lebesgue integral divided by the rectangle's volume.

The maximal and overlap functions may take the value infinity. Lean represents their values by ℝ≥0∞, meaning \([0,\infty]\); here ∫⁻, ∑' and ⨆ denote the nonnegative integral, sum and supremum. ENNReal.ofReal places a nonnegative real number in this extended range. The exp used for overlap bounds is the usual exponential, with value infinity at infinity.

In these statements, eLpNorm denotes the \(L^p\) norm with respect to Lebesgue measure, allowing infinity. For \(0<p<\infty\), a finite value means precisely that the function is Lebesgue measurable and the integral of \(|f|^p\) is finite. MemLp asserts that this norm is finite and so expresses membership in \(L^p\). LocallyIntegrable means membership in \(L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}\).

When the input is integrable on each rectangle, the maximal function is proved to agree with the supremum formed using ordinary real integrals.

The sharpness statement uses finite families and ordinary real integrals. The overlap exponential has a finite integral on the shadow. Tendsto (fun N => a N) atTop atTop expresses \(a_N\to+\infty\) as \(N\to\infty\).

Boundary conventions

The explicit shifted grids in the paper use \([a,b)\) in each scalar coordinate. cubeIco, rectangleIco and maximalIco use that convention directly. The prescribed-scale covering asserts IcoGrid, which records the partition and nesting properties of the same cube corners with these boundaries. The rounded-slice identity uses sliceFamilyIco and overlapIco, so its equality holds at every point with this convention.

Mathlib's boxes use \((a,b]\). For locally integrable inputs, Lean proves that the resulting maximal functions agree almost everywhere for the rounded family and at every point for the continuous family. The continuous maximal function is measurable.

Dyadic averages and square functions

SquareFunctions.lean defines the dyadic operators in the grouped coordinates above. The notation follows Supporting and additional results.

cubeMean is the signed average \(\langle u\rangle_Q\), and cubeAverage is the operator \(E_Q u=\mathbf1_Q\langle u\rangle_Q\). cubeDifference is \(\Delta_Q u=\sum_{J\in\operatorname{ch}(Q)}E_Ju-E_Qu\). It uses Mathlib's midpoint subdivision; for grid cubes, this is proved to give exactly the dyadic children.

coordinateAverage and coordinateDifference apply these operators in one coordinate block while holding the others fixed. partialAverage and partialDifference compose the operators in the selected coordinates in increasing index order. The empty product is the identity. Selecting all coordinates gives fullAverage and fullDifference.

signedMaximal is the supremum of \(|\langle g\rangle_R|\) over grid rectangles containing the point, with the absolute value after the average. squareFunction is the square root of the sum of \(|\Delta_Rg|^2\) over the whole grid. Both functions take values in \([0,\infty]\). productMaximal gives the usual supremum of averages of \(|f|\) in grouped coordinates.

For the weighted estimates, a top rectangle and a smallest side length \(2^{-k}\) determine finite grids. bottomCubes are the cubes of that smallest size; interiorCubes are the larger grid cubes inside the top factor. The constancy conditions require the input to be constant on each bottom cube or product of bottom cubes. partialSquareFunction is the square root of the sum of squared differences in selected coordinates over these interior cubes. combinedSquareFunction is \(Wg=(\sum_j(S_{A_j}g)^2)^{1/2}\), where \(A_j\) omits coordinate \(j\). The region partialRectangleRegion is \(K^A\times I_0^{A^c}\): selected factors come from \(K\), and the others are the top factors.

The projection and its averaging families

Projections.lean uses the same coordinate operators in the finite dyadic setting. finiteCubes and finiteRectangles include the smallest cubes.

For a family \(\mathcal G\) in this finite grid, projection defines \(F_{\mathcal G}\) by subtracting from \(f\) the product martingale differences indexed by interior rectangles contained in members of \(\mathcal G\). removedRectangles is a set, so each removed rectangle is counted once. Under the finite-setting hypotheses these sums have finitely many terms; the notation ∑' is therefore an ordinary finite sum. For an input supported on the top rectangle and constant on its bottom rectangles, the full-grid formula agrees with the finite construction.

To define a cross-coordinate average, fix the cubes in all coordinates except \(j\). crossCandidates collects the $jth factors of members of \(\mathcal G\) containing those fixed cubes in the other coordinates, and includes every bottom cube of the top factor. crossPartition selects the maximal candidates, using the box order P ≤ Q for containment. They partition the top factor, and crossAverage averages over this partition in coordinate \(j\).

averagingRectangles is the family \(\mathcal B\): rectangles in the finite grid with at least one factor belonging to the cross partition determined by their other factors. These are the averaging rectangles used in the projected-function estimates.
