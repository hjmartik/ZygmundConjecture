The Zygmund and Rey conjectures in Lean

What this is

A Lean 4 formalization accompanying Henri Martikainen's The Zygmund conjecture and Rey's exponential integrability conjecture, built on Mathlib.

The formalization covers the two conjectures and their underlying maximal estimate, together with related dyadic and overlap estimates and a sparse-family sharpness result. The paper is the primary mathematical exposition.

Main results

The paper proves the Zygmund conjecture in all parameters, in the following continuous form.

Let \(m\geq3\), let \(d_1,\ldots,d_m\geq1\), and set \(d=d_1+\cdots+d_m\). We work on \(\mathbb R^{d_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb R^{d_m}=\mathbb R^d\) with Lebesgue measure. Let \(\phi:(0,\infty)^{m-1}\to(0,\infty)\) be coordinatewise nondecreasing. Let \(\mathcal R_\phi\) consist of all rectangles \(R=R^1\times\cdots\times R^m\) with axis-parallel cube factors \(R^i\subset\mathbb R^{d_i}\) and side-length tuple

\[(s_1,\ldots,s_{m-1},\phi(s_1,\ldots,s_{m-1})), \qquad s_1,\ldots,s_{m-1}>0.\]

For a family \(\mathcal E\) of rectangles, define

\[M_{\mathcal E}f(x) =\sup_{\substack{I\in\mathcal E\\x\in I}} \frac1{|I|}\int_I |f(y)|\,dy.\]

Theorem (Zygmund conjecture). For every \(f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^d)\) and every \(\lambda>0\),

\[|\{x:M_{\mathcal R_\phi}f(x)>\lambda\}| \leq C_{m,d}\int_{\mathbb R^d}\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda} \left[\log\!\left(e+\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\right]^{m-2}dx.\]

The constant depends only on \(m\) and \(d\), not on \(\phi\). No continuity or doubling assumption on \(\phi\) is imposed. This weak \(L(\log L)^{m-2}\) estimate saves one logarithm compared with the full \(m\)-parameter strong maximal function, whose logarithmic power is \(m-1\).

The central analytic estimate is \(\|M_{\mathcal G}f\|_p\leq C_{m,d}(p')^{m-1}\|f\|_p\), \(1<p\leq2\), for weakly incomparable \(m\)-parameter dyadic rectangle families, \(m\geq2\). Here \(p'=p/(p-1)\). Weak incomparability means that no two rectangles are strictly nested in every coordinate factor. The estimate saves one factor of \(p'\) compared with the unrestricted product maximal operator. Duality gives exponential integrability of the overlap function \(h_{\mathcal G}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal G}\mathbf 1_I\), with power \(1/(m-1)\), for sparse weakly incomparable families, and in particular proves Rey's conjecture.

Weak incomparability also accommodates the slices arising after dyadic rounding in the proof of the continuous Zygmund theorem.

Independent specification, verification and correspondence with the paper

Main theorems presents the six results in mathematical prose. Their precise formal meaning is given by an independent specification rebuilt from Mathlib primitives, without importing the proof library. The specification states the results and defines the mathematical objects needed to interpret them. No software installation is needed to inspect these statements and definitions.

Mathematical review checked that the statements and their definitions express the intended results of the paper. This addresses the central question: does the Lean specification faithfully represent the mathematics we intend to prove? The formal checks then verify that the supplied proofs establish those statements.

The mathematical arguments are developed separately in the proof library, using its own definitions and supporting constructions.

Bridges proved in Lean connect those definitions to the independent specification. The public proofs use these bridges to derive the six independently stated results from the library’s theorems. The consistency guide explains these connections and the mathematical meaning of constructions used within the proofs.

Comparator compares each resulting theorem with its independent specification, including the definitions determining its meaning. All six comparisons passed. Lean’s default kernel, Nanoda and con-ron accepted the exported proofs and their exported dependencies. The theorem proofs contain no omitted steps (sorry) and use only the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The verification guide records the checking scope and reproduction instructions.

A checked proof of a faithful specification establishes the intended mathematical results; whether it follows the paper’s argument is a separate question. This formalization was developed to follow the paper’s proof strategies closely. Separate mathematical reviews examined that correspondence and found agreement in the main lines of argument. The consistency guide explains how the paper’s definitions and supporting constructions are represented in Lean.

Building

The development uses Lean 4.35.0-rc4 and a pinned Mathlib version. See the verification guide for dependency setup, build options and checking commands.

For ordinary compilation after that setup:

cd lean
lake build

How this was built

The formalization was developed with AI assistance, with separate reviews of its scope and faithfulness to the intended mathematics. The main Lean codebase was produced using GPT-6-Astra in a workflow with specialized subagents. Claude Fable 5.1 provided additional independent and blind reviews of the mathematical specifications and of the correspondence between the formal proofs and the paper’s main arguments.
