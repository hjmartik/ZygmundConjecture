Independently inspectable mathematical statements

This independent specification is rebuilt from Mathlib primitives. The six propositions in SubmittedStatements.lean use only Mathlib and the definitions in Definitions.lean and SubmittedDefinitions.lean. None of these files imports the proof library. The statements and all definitions needed to interpret them can therefore be inspected without reading that library.

The results concern Henri Martikainen's The Zygmund conjecture and Rey's exponential integrability conjecture. The central statements are the weakly incomparable maximal estimate, Rey's overlap estimate, and the continuous Zygmund endpoint. The rounded endpoint supports the continuous result; the other two statements concern overlap for sparse Φ-Zygmund families and sharpness for the classical dyadic Zygmund family.

The propositions and their proofs are in the namespace ReyZygmund.MathlibOnly.Submitted. SubmittedSolutions.lean proves the six statements using the proof library and the connections described below.

The maximal statement uses the paper's 1 < p ≤ 2 range. The underlying theorem proves the same weakly incomparable estimate for every real p > 1.

The maximal function and overlap are defined using nonnegative extended real values, which may be infinite. The L^p maximal and sparse overlap statements also assert that these quantities are finite almost everywhere under their hypotheses. The real-valued functions used in the norms and exponential integrals therefore agree with them outside a set of measure zero.

For the classical dyadic Zygmund family, the sharpness statement retains a stronger quantified form of the paper's remark. For m ≥ 3, 0 < eta < 1 and every choice of dyadic grids, it gives a sequence of finite incomparable eta-sparse families G_N, N ≥ 2, whose unions have measure one. For every c > 0 and beta > 1/(m-1), the integrals of exp(c h^beta) over these unions tend to infinity, where h is the overlap function of the corresponding family. The same sequence works for all such c and beta.

Connections to the proof library

The independent grid definition uses cubes of side length 2^k which partition the whole space at each integer scale k, with intersecting cubes nested. The proof library uses the usual dyadic children obtained by halving each side. GridBridge.lean proves that the two grid descriptions are equivalent for arbitrary grids, keeping the cubes unchanged and relating the scale indices by n = -k. Through this equivalence, the constructed shifted grids also give examples of the independent grid definition.

Weak incomparability rules out strict containment in every cube factor. SubmittedBridges.lean proves that this is equivalent to the condition used in the proof library: when one rectangle contains another, at least one cube factor is unchanged (HasEqualFactorOnContainment).

The independent sparsity definition allows Lebesgue-measurable subsets of the rectangles. Here NullMeasurableSet expresses measurability for completed Lebesgue measure. The proof library uses Borel subsets. SubmittedMeasure.lean proves that each of the subsets in the sparsity definition can be replaced by a Borel subset of the same measure, preserving containment, pairwise disjointness and the exact sparsity parameter.

The independent maximal operator is the supremum of averages of |f|, defined using nonnegative integrals that may be infinite. For locally integrable f, OperatorBridge.lean proves that these averages agree with the ordinary real-valued averages used in the proofs, and hence define the same maximal function. The hypotheses of the maximal estimates supply the required integrability on each rectangle.

The same file identifies the explicitly defined shifted cubes and rounded families with those used in the proofs. Different original side lengths can give the same first m-1 rounded scales but different last scales; all these tuples are retained. FamilyBridge.lean proves that the two grid descriptions give the same families of dyadic rectangles and the same families satisfying the prescribed relations between side lengths.

Mathlib's boxes use (a,b] in each scalar coordinate, while the paper's shifted grids use [a,b). For locally integrable f, BoundaryBridge.lean proves that the continuous maximal functions for the two conventions agree at every point, using continuity of the rectangle integrals under translation. For the countable rounded family, the maximal functions agree almost everywhere. The same file proves that the continuous maximal function is measurable. The rounded statement uses [a,b) directly; SubmittedMeasure.lean proves that changing the convention preserves the measures of its maximal-function level sets.

Some intermediate estimates are written for the exponential minus one. SubmittedBridges.lean converts these to the exponential bounds in the statements by adding the measure of the shadow.

For each finite family in the sharpness construction, SubmittedMeasure.lean proves that the exponential is integrable and that its nonnegative and ordinary real integrals agree. The sharpness statement uses the real integrals: each is finite, and these finite values tend to infinity along the sequence for the coefficients and powers specified in the statement.

Verification and mathematical review

The six statements and their definitions were independently reviewed against the paper. Their proofs passed the recorded local Lean checks: compilation, comparison of registered declarations with their separately written formal statements, checks of the axioms used by the proofs and their dependencies, and a further kernel check of the selected compiled modules.

All six independently stated main results passed Comparator comparison, and their exported proofs and exported dependencies were accepted by Lean's default kernel, Nanoda and con-ron. Supporting results outside those exports are covered by the ordinary Lean checks described in the verification guide; their presence in the library does not imply separate Nanoda or con-ron verification.

Mathematical review assesses whether the specification expresses the intended mathematics. Comparator checks that the supplied theorems match the independent specification, including its definitions. Lean, Nanoda and con-ron check the exported proofs and their exported dependencies.
