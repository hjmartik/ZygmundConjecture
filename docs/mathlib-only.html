Independently inspectable mathematical statements

This independent specification is rebuilt from Mathlib primitives. Statements.lean gives the three main results; SupportingStatements.lean gives nineteen supporting and additional results. The geometric objects, rectangle families and maximal and overlap functions are defined in Definitions.lean. SquareFunctions.lean defines the averages, martingale differences and square functions; Projections.lean defines the finite projection and its averaging families. These five files import no proof-library module.

To inspect one result, read its statement and the definitions listed below it. The correctness of the other specifications is not a prerequisite for that result.

The results concern Henri Martikainen's The Zygmund conjecture and Rey's exponential integrability conjecture. Their mathematical statements appear in Main theorems and Supporting and additional results.

The definition guide explains how these familiar geometric objects and dyadic operators are represented in Lean, including the conventions for coordinates, boundaries and integrals. The verification guide records the checking scope, results and reproduction instructions.

Maximal–square-function estimate

  • Geometry: Rectangle, CubeGrid, gridRectangles, ProductPoint, productRectangle.
  • Martingale differences: cubeMean, cubeAverage, cubeDifference, coordinateDifference, PartialRectangle, partialDifference, fullDifference.
  • Maximal and square functions: signedMaximal, squareFunction.

The maximal function takes the absolute value after the signed average. The square function and maximal function use the whole grid.

Scalar estimate

Only real numbers, their order, arithmetic and real powers are used.

One-coordinate weighted square-function estimate

CubeGrid, cubeMean, cubeAverage, cubeDifference, bottomCubes, interiorCubes, ConstantOnBottomCubes.

The smallest side length is 2^(-k). The sum excludes that bottom scale; the functions are constant on its cubes, and the weight is strictly positive.

Product weighted square-function estimate

  • Geometry: Rectangle, CubeGrid, ProductPoint, productRectangle, PartialRectangle.
  • Coordinate operators: cubeMean, cubeAverage, cubeDifference, coordinateAverage, coordinateDifference, partialAverage, partialDifference.
  • Finite grids and integration regions: bottomCubes, interiorCubes, ConstantOnBottomRectangles, partialInterior, partialRectangleRegion.

The same cutoff is used in every coordinate. partialAverage and partialDifference compose the coordinate operators in increasing index order. The integration region uses the selected cubes and the remaining top factors. The partial average in the denominator remains a function of the unselected coordinates.

Common projection definitions

The projection identities and estimates use the following definitions:

  • Geometry: Rectangle, CubeGrid, ProductPoint, productRectangle, PartialRectangle.
  • Martingale differences: cubeMean, cubeAverage, cubeDifference, coordinateDifference, partialDifference, fullDifference.
  • Finite grids and the input: bottomCubes, interiorCubes, ConstantOnBottomRectangles, finiteCubes, finiteRectangles.
  • Projection: removedRectangles, projection.

The input function is positive on the top rectangle, zero outside it, and constant on products of smallest cubes. The projection subtracts each removed martingale difference once and does not need to be positive.

Averages in fewer coordinates

Read the common projection definitions, together with coordinateAverage, partialAverage and fullAverage.

Finset.univ.ssubsets lists the proper subsets of all coordinates. The empty subset is included; its averaging operator is the identity. The formula holds on each rectangle of the family, without an incomparability hypothesis.

Differences of the projected function

Read the common projection definitions, together with coordinateAverage, crossCandidates, crossPartition and crossAverage.

Finset.univ.erase j selects all coordinates except j. The partial rectangle has exactly these factors, each of which may be a smallest cube. The cross average acts in the omitted coordinate. The identity is pointwise everywhere.

Unchanged projection and weak incomparability

Read the common projection definitions, together with WeaklyIncomparable, crossCandidates, crossPartition and averagingRectangles.

The enlarged family is the union of the original family and the averaging rectangles. The enlargement leaves the projection unchanged. If the original family is weakly incomparable, so is the enlarged family.

Representation of the averages

Read the definitions for unchanged projection and weak incomparability, together with coordinateAverage, partialAverage and fullAverage.

The full average on the left acts on f; the partial averages on the right act on its projection. The identity holds on every rectangle of the enlarged family.

Reduction to square functions

Read the definitions for unchanged projection and weak incomparability, together with productMaximal, partialInterior, partialSquareFunction and combinedSquareFunction.

productMaximal is the supremum of averages of |f| in grouped coordinates. combinedSquareFunction is W F: the square root of the sum of squares of the m partial square functions obtained by omitting one coordinate at a time.

Square-function estimate for the projected function

Read the common projection definitions, together with crossCandidates, crossPartition, averagingRectangles, productMaximal, partialInterior, partialSquareFunction and combinedSquareFunction.

The maximal function in the denominator uses only the averaging rectangles. This estimate does not require weak incomparability.

Prescribed-scale covering

CubeGrid, rounding, shiftedCubes, cubeIco, IcoGrid.

The family of grids is indexed by Fin d → Fin 3, giving three choices in each of the d scalar coordinates. The grids are chosen before the cube to be covered. The covering cube belongs to scale rounding s, so its side is exactly 2 ^ rounding s. The closed input cube is covered, including its boundary; IcoGrid records the grid properties for [lower, upper) cubes.

Córdoba–Fefferman selection

Point, Rectangle, CubeGrid, rectangle, gridRectangles, shadow.

F.Finite says that the family is finite. G \ {R} removes R from the selected family. The constant depends only on the number of factors.

Weak incomparability of slices

Point, Rectangle, rectangleIco, cubeIco, initialProjection, appendLastPoint, overlapIco, sliceFamilyIco, rounding, roundedScales, shiftedCubes, roundedRectangles.

appendLastPoint x t is the point (x,t). The slice is a set of projected rectangles. Incomparability and weak incomparability are written out using the same [lower, upper) sets as the slices and overlap. ∃! R means that exactly one original rectangle has the stated properties.

Half-sparsity of slices

Point, Rectangle, CubeGrid, rectangle, gridRectangles, shadow, initialProjection, sliceFamily, Sparse.

The family is finite and satisfies the displayed overlap condition. For each slice, Sparse asserts the existence of the required disjoint measurable sets.
